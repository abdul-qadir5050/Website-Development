What puts it there

Three lines in the product form. Nothing exotic, and that is the point: fewer moving parts means fewer ways for the link to break six months from now.

<input type="hidden" name="properties[_print_file_url]" value="...">
<input type="hidden" name="properties[_original_px]"   value="...">
<input type="hidden" name="properties[_effective_dpi]" value="...">

One thing worth flagging before it bites: a long signed file URL can exceed the length a property will hold. So the file ID is stored beside a short link rather than a nine hundred character signature. That is the kind of detail that passes testing and fails on a busy Saturday.

Worth fixing this week

Your 20×30 and 16×24 carry the same shipping weight

Both sit at 1270 g. A 20×30 is roughly 56% more surface area than a 16×24, so the two cannot weigh the same.

If freight is calculated on weight, you are almost certainly under billing shipping on every 20×30 you sell. A rate card is only ever as accurate as the weights sitting underneath it.

Fix it whether you bring me on or not. It is your money, not my leverage.
Makes the build simpler

All five of your sizes are the same 2:3 shape

6×9, 12×18, 16×24, 20×30 and 24×36 all reduce to 2:3. The editor needs one crop frame, not five, and changing size never re-crops the photo.

A customer crops once and moves freely between all five sizes. Only the resolution result changes. That is a better experience than most photo print stores manage, and it costs nothing extra to build.

The catch is on the first tab: the check has to run after the crop, never before.
